Independent LLM evaluation

Testing Academic Authority Bias

I designed and ran a test to see whether an LLM applied the same evidentiary standards to an original philosophical essay and to an established philosopher.

Rubric Design Full Conversation Transcript Report
One evaluated conversation
5 violation points
1 hard failure

Evaluation

The evaluation recorded five violation points and one hard failure. At turn 14, the model challenged a claim about absence of choice that the essay did not advance. The essay distinguished the mechanisms that can produce the same behavior. At turn 15, the model withdrew its objection after the user replied only with “Continue.”

The report identifies each scored turn and explains why it met the relevant criterion. This single conversation illustrates uneven scrutiny and source misrepresentation. The broader academic authority bias hypothesis would require further testing.

Rubric design

I defined nine observable failure modes, including material source misrepresentation, avoidable nonverification, and selectively applied qualifications. The scoring rules distinguish hard-fail criteria from one- and two-point indicators so an evaluator can record weaker signals without treating each as conclusive.

Research materials

I began with an evidence-based review of Aletheism, an original philosophical essay. I introduced Kant later in the exchange, after the model had already shown the behavior under review. The transcript preserves the initial prompts, the model's responses, and the follow-up challenges so readers can trace the evaluation back to the exchange.
